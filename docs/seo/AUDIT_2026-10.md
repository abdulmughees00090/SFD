## How it was tested
- 47 pages re-scanned: 0 broken links, 0 duplicate titles/descriptions/H1, 0 JSON-LD errors, 0 mixed content.
- 66 behavioural tests on both API routes and the service worker, all pass.
- Before/after proof: crash on empty body, formula stored verbatim, bad email accepted, 2 MB message stored, `URIError` on `%`, HTML injected via toast: all reproduced on the old code and gone on the new.
- Headless Chromium, 1440px and 375px, original vs fixed: careers, contact, products and an article are pixel-identical; no JS errors. Homepage differs only inside an animated hero region that also differs between two runs of the original.

## Owner decisions applied (2026-10-11)
1. **Demo logins** on `/products/` stay exactly as they are (public demo accounts, no real data).
2. **Unproven claims removed**: the `/about/` case study section and its schema (12 locations, -25% costs, 3 months, 99.9% uptime); every "50,000+ downloads" (homepage, its schema, `/services/mobile-apps/` title/description/body) now says "published on the Google Play Store"; the "Proven track record" and "Proven Case Study" badges became "Published application" and "Client Project"; "Unmatched Support / 24/7 assistance" on `/about/` became "Dedicated Support / Ongoing assistance"; the unsourced "over 80% of digital transactions in Ghana" became a non-numeric statement. No section layout was disturbed; the About page simply flows from "Advantage" straight to the closing call to action.
3. **Twitter/X**: all 37 `twitter:site` / `twitter:creator` tags removed from 36 pages. The `twitter:card/title/description/image` tags stay; they are the generic share-preview tags that other platforms also read and do not name an account.
4. **Nora Eve Bridal** kept as a Shopify bridal boutique store built by your developer. URL updated to the live domain `https://noraeve.co.uk/` (the old domain redirects) in `products`, `company-facts` and `llms.txt`, wording "Bridal boutique Shopify store".
5. **Images**: 12 images over 20 KB (6 hero images, CEO photo, 5 product logos) now ship as WebP with the original PNG as fallback via `<picture>`; 857 KB -> 348 KB (about 59% smaller). A `picture { display: contents }` rule guarantees no layout change. Verified in Chromium at 1440 px and 375 px: image positions and page heights are identical on every page whose text was not edited; only compression noise differs. The small header logos were left as PNG (7-13 KB on every page, no real gain).

## Still worth confirming (left unchanged, lower risk)
- Operational promises written as fact: "24/7 monitoring" (`/services/website-maintenance/`, `/privacy/`), "we respond within 24 hours" (`/contact/`, `/services/`), "review within 5 business days" (`/apply/`). Keep only if the team really meets them.
- "13+ years" experience in the CEO bio and schema (`/ceo-message/`).
- Careers stats bar: "100% Remote-friendly", "100% Values-driven" are slogans, not measurements; consider rewording.
- Third-party statistics without a cited source (WordPress "over 40% of the web", "53% of mobile users abandon", "up to 70%" WebP savings). They are broadly in line with public figures but unsourced.
- `keywords` and `geo.*` meta tags on careers are ignored by Google and Bing; harmless.
- `scripts/optimize_images.js` still describes the older image workflow; the new WebP files were generated with the same `sharp` dependency at the PNGs' native size.
